Most of the delays I get called in to fix didn't start in development. They started in the first two weeks, when nobody wrote down what "done" actually meant.

## The pattern

A stakeholder describes what they want in a meeting. Someone takes notes. Engineering starts building from those notes. Six weeks later, the demo doesn't match what the stakeholder pictured, and now you're rebuilding under time pressure.

Nobody did anything wrong. There just wasn't a shared definition to point at.

## What I do differently

Before any build starts, I run a requirements workshop and produce a Functional Specification Document — a plain-language description of what the system does, agreed by both the client and the engineering team.

It sounds bureaucratic. It saves weeks.

- Stakeholders see exactly what they're getting before money is spent
- Engineers stop guessing at intent
- Scope changes become visible decisions instead of silent drift

## The takeaway

If your project is slipping, look at what was agreed in week one. Most of the time, the answer is there.
